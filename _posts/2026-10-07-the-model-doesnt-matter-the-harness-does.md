The model doesn't matter. The harness does.

This is a follow-up to [Let's Talk About "Claude"]({% post_url 2026-07-20-lets-talk-about-claude %}). In that piece I split "Claude" into two parts: the harness, the experience on top of the model, and the models themselves. Chat, Cowork, Code. Connectors, skills, the loop you live in. Most of what people mean when they say they love Claude is the harness. The models are great. They are not the product you feel.

I've only gotten blunter since I wrote it.

The model doesn't matter. Everything since Opus 4.8-ish has been good enough for day-to-day tasks, outside of the hardest engineering and cyber problems. Ordinary work doesn't need a fresh argument about which lab won the week. A model from this stretch will do the job. The exception is real, and it's narrow: the hardest engineering, and the hardest cyber problems. If that isn't the task on your desk, you are past the part of the curve where swapping models is the move.

The agent harness is what matters. Context. Connectors. Heartbeats. Browser use. The machinery that puts a model inside the work, instead of inside a tab you have to remember to open.

Here's what that looks like for me right now.

Muse, Instinct, GrokBot, and Dot for personal work. incident.io for production reliability. Optimizely and Profound for marketing. Cognition, Claude Code, and SpaceX Super Intelligence for engineering. And so on.

Read it as a list of jobs, not a list of models. I didn't rank Opus against anything else in that stack. I named the places the work already lives. Personal work is not production reliability. Marketing is not engineering. Each of those wants its own context, its own connections, and its own way of running. One chat box asked to be all of them turns into pasting the same background into every thread and calling it a system.

Context is the piece that decides whether the harness is telling the truth about your company. If the agent can't see the source of truth, it will write a fluent version of your product, your pricing, and your voice from whatever it can guess. Connectors are how you stop being the person who copies from the system of record into a prompt. Heartbeats are the difference between an agent and a search box with manners: something happens when you aren't hovering. Browser use, or whatever the equivalent is where your work lives, is how a task finishes instead of ending as a paragraph you're supposed to go execute by hand.

I think the leverage point these days is taking full advantage of the harness.

That means filling in the one you have. The context. The connections. A reason for it to run when you aren't in the room. The ability to take a step, not just describe the step. Waiting on the next model drop is a way to feel busy while the harness sits half-wired. Collecting logins is the same habit in a new tab.

The leverage isn't a model argument. It's a harness that knows the business. Different harness for personal work, for reliability, for marketing, for engineering. Good-enough models underneath. The advantage shows up in the work, because the tool can see what you're doing and can keep going.

If the Claude piece sounded right to you, don't file it under commentary and move on. "Claude" was mostly a harness story then. It's more of one now. The model cleared the bar for day-to-day work somewhere around Opus 4.8. Use that. Then take full advantage of the harness, because context, connectors, heartbeats, and browser use are the product from here.

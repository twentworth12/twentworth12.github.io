Sales engineers know the customer best.

Not marketing. Not the CMO. Not the account executive who just got off a call and is sure the whole market moved before lunch.

Matt Heinz prompted this for me. He has a view that CMOs should own the entire revenue function, in part because marketing knows the customer better than anyone else in the building. His line: not should, does.

I've spent just about 50% of my career in sales and 50% in marketing, and my perspective is the opposite. Marketing does not know the customer better than anyone else.

We have a perspective, and it's a useful one. We see the customer at scale, gathered mostly from indirect channels. We read call transcripts. We read reviews. We stitch a pattern out of artifacts other people made while they were in the room. A good marketing team is better at that stitching than any single rep will be. It is still secondhand. A transcript is what the conversation left behind. It is not the conversation.

Nothing replaces what you learn on the front lines. Sitting on discovery calls. Guiding customers through POVs. Talking to the CFO about ROI. That's where you find out which promise the buyer repeated back, and which one they let pass. That's where a POV shows you what the customer tries when the slide is over. A transcript can't carry that. Reading about the meeting is not the meeting.

So if marketing doesn't know the customer the best, who does?

AEs are often unreliable narrators. They have a ton of context. They also have a short-term memory, and their perspective is skewed by recency bias. Whatever the last prospect said is what every prospect said. I don't think they're inventing it. The last deal is the one in front of them, so it becomes the market. The last objection becomes every objection. The last thing a champion loved becomes the whole pitch. The story changes, the pitch changes with it, and marketing is downstream of a mood.

In my experience, the best source of unbiased customer intelligence is the SE organization.

I was an SE for the early part of my career, and I got moved into marketing because I knew way more about the customer than the marketing team did. That's the whole lesson, and it wasn't a compliment to my branding skills. I had been in the deals. SEs sit where the technical conversation and the commercial conversation are the same conversation. They run the POV. They hear the same question from companies that have never met each other, which is how a pattern separates itself from a one-off. They are close enough to the product, and close enough to the buyer, to know when a story we're telling won't survive the room.

They're also harder to spin. An AE lives inside the commit. A good SE has to live inside what the product will do in the customer's environment. That gap is where unbiased intelligence comes from. Not from a persona doc. From someone who has watched the same moment land, or die, across a lot of accounts, and isn't rewriting history to protect the forecast.

Marketing still has a job, and I'm not quitting it. Somebody has to turn what the field knows into a point of view the market can repeat. Somebody has to decide what we say, who we say it to, and what we refuse to say. We get worse at that when we treat our own research as the source and the people on the front lines as an audience we brief. Flip it. The SEs are the source. Marketing is the craft of saying it clearly, and the discipline not to amplify the last anecdote an AE walked in with.

Maybe the revenue function should roll into SEs. I'm more serious about that than it sounds. If the case for a CMO owning revenue rests on "marketing knows the customer," and that sentence is wrong, then a lot of confident operating models are standing on a bad assumption. Put the people who know the customer closest to the decisions about what we sell and what we say. Then let marketing do marketing.

Maybe that's why I enjoy working so closely with my partner and former SE, Mohit Bijlani. He still has the habit of someone who had to sit with a customer and make the product make sense, live, with no edit pass afterward. I'll take that over a point of view built only from transcripts.

If you run marketing, get the customer story from your SEs before you write it. Ask what buyers say when you aren't there. Write down the pattern, not the last call. That's the customer.
